FACEBOOK SEO + WRITING RULES (distilled from the SEO skills: core ranking, topical map, entity/semantic, AIO/GEO, SERP features, QC rubric)

These rules sit on top of the grounding rules. If a rule below would force you to state something the SOURCE TEXT does not say, drop the rule, never the accuracy.

1. DIRECT ANSWER FIRST. The first sentence (under 120 characters) must answer "what is this place, where is it, why is it famous" and carry the single most surprising fact the source states. It contains the place name and the country. A reader (or a search engine) must understand the post from line one.
2. SEARCH WORDS. People search in plain questions: where is it, why is it famous, how old is it, how big is it, when was it built. Answer such questions naturally inside the caption, but only those the SOURCE TEXT can answer.
3. ENTITY DENSITY. Name at least 6 real entities that the SOURCE TEXT states: country, region or city, nearby landmark, river or mountain, builder, dynasty or architect, UNESCO or protection status, historical period. Disambiguate any name that could mean two things.
4. INFORMATION GAIN. Include at least one detail that a typical "top 10 places" list would skip: an odd measurement, a construction detail, a forgotten purpose, a record, a rule. Prefer the lesser-known fact over the famous one.
5. STAT + SOURCE + IMPLICATION. Include one sentence with a number taken from the SOURCE TEXT and one short consequence for a reader or visitor. No arithmetic, no conversion.
6. MODULAR PARAGRAPHS. 3 to 5 short paragraphs, 25 to 60 words each, every paragraph self-contained: one claim, one piece of evidence, one bit of context. If the source gives two comparable numbers, a one-line comparison is welcome.
7. TOPICAL FOCUS. Stay inside the page niche: places a traveller can visit. When the source names another attraction nearby or in the same family, mention it by name. Never wander into politics, celebrities, or news.
8. TRUST SIGNALS. Never fake first-hand experience: no "I visited", no invented traveller quotes. Credibility comes from precise, sourced facts. Practical visitor information (season, access, entry, opening) only if the SOURCE TEXT states it.
9. FACEBOOK ENGAGEMENT-BAIT RULES. No "like, share, comment, tag a friend" demands, no "comment YES", no cliffhanger the post does not resolve. End with ONE genuine, easy question about the place. Vary the structure from post to post.
10. HASHTAGS. Exactly 2: the place name as one word (for example #MachuPicchu) and one topical tag such as #WorldTravel. No spaces, no more.
11. QUALITY GATE. If the article has no surprising, source-stated fact, return skip.
